# Technical writing

Independent adaptation, not a Google-certified skill. Editing grants no additional publication, deletion or execution authority.

1. Identify the reader, next decision and canonical source. Inspect required machine-readable fields before editing.
2. Lead with the result/action. Use one idea per sentence, explicit actors and active verbs. Order by reader need, not conversational history.
3. Remove filler, repeated history and obsolete instructions. Keep each changing rule in one source and link to it. Preserve unique decisions in recoverable history outside routine context.
4. Keep hashes, IDs, authorization boundaries, uncertainties, acceptance evidence and unresolved findings. Never turn a qualified result into success or drop defects to satisfy a length target.
5. Use Japanese for human conclusions and English for AI-only instructions; avoid full bilingual copies. Follow docs/pr-review-loop.md for project comments. Report the delta and link logs.
6. Compare before/after for lost meaning and broken links. Measure actual tokenizer usage before claiming token savings; characters and HTML folding are not token savings. Missing mandatory source means stop dependent actions.

Sources: Google's [audience](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience), [document structure](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/documents), [short sentences](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/short-sentences), and [active voice](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/active-voice).
